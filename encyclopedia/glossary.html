Town of Salem Glossary: Claims, CC, VFR, Wills & Common Terms

A fast glossary for Town of Salem vocabulary, social deduction shorthand, voting terms, and night-action language.

For Town of Salem abbreviations, keep the meaning tied to the current role list and explain unfamiliar shorthand when pressure is high.

ClaimA public statement of your role or role category.
CC / CounterclaimA claim that competes for the same limited role slot.
VFRVote for Role: pressure used to force claims or information.
WillA written night log that can be revealed or read after death.
InnoVote or judgment meaning innocent / do not eliminate.
GuiltyVote or judgment to eliminate the player on trial.
SusShort for suspicious; often used for investigative or behavioral concern.
ConfirmedSupported strongly enough by mechanics or public events that the table treats the identity as highly reliable.
WagonA cluster of votes or pressure accumulating on one player.
Soft claimHinting at a role or role class without fully revealing it.
Hard claimExplicitly stating the exact role.
RoleblockAn effect that prevents or disrupts another player’s intended night action.
VisitA night interaction in which one role targets another player.
ParityA board state where an evil faction can equal or control the Town vote count.
ScumreadA behavioral read that a player may belong to an evil faction.
TownreadA behavioral or mechanical read that a player is likely aligned with Town.

Claim and CC

A claim is the role and action history a player says they have. CC means counterclaim: another player asserts a role or slot that competes with the first claim. A counterclaim is only mechanically decisive when the setup cannot legally contain both stories.

VFR and pressure

VFR means Vote for Role. Players use votes to force a role claim from an unclaimed slot, then back off or continue depending on the answer. Pressure is broader: any question or wagon intended to make a player commit to testable information.

Scumread, townread and POE

A scumread is a behavioral or strategic suspicion; a townread is the opposite. POE—process of elimination—narrows suspects by removing players with stronger confirmation. Reads are not the same thing as mechanical results.

Parity and kingmaker

Parity describes a state where an evil faction controls enough votes or kills to prevent Town from recovering. A kingmaker is a player, often neutral, whose choice effectively decides which faction wins even if that player’s own objective differs.

Bus, sheep and tunnel

To bus is to push or vote a teammate, often for credibility. To sheep is to follow another player’s read without adding independent reasoning. To tunnel is to stay fixed on one target despite new information that should reopen the analysis.

Visit, roleblock, transport and control

A visit is movement caused by many active night abilities. A roleblock prevents an action; transport swaps action destinations; control redirects another player’s target. These terms describe different mechanics and should not be used interchangeably when resolving a night.
