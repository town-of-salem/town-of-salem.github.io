🧠 Town of Salem strategy

Town of Salem Target Priority

Choose better night targets by information value, threat, confirmability and expected reactions.

Quick answer: Town of Salem role priority is situational rather than fixed. The best target is the player whose result changes a decision. Rank targets by information value, threat, likelihood of being attacked and whether another role can verify the outcome.

Investigative priority: resolve disputed slots

Checking an already-confirmed Town member usually adds little. Investigate players whose claim occupies a contested role slot, whose result group can separate two stories, or whose alignment changes today’s vote.

Protective priority: predict the kill

Doctor and Bodyguard should think like attackers. Who can expose evil tomorrow? Who holds confirmed vote power? Who just posted a result that will become dangerous after one more night? Protect the likely target, not simply the most famous role.

Control priority: stop the action that matters

Tavern Keeper, Jailor and other control roles gain value when they deny a specific action. Blocking a quiet passive claim may do nothing; blocking a suspected hostile killer or controller may create safety and information. Blocking a credible Town investigator or protector instead needs a specific reason.

Killing priority: remove future leverage

Mafia and independent killers should evaluate what the victim would do tomorrow, not how annoying the victim was today. A player linking multiple claims can be a higher-value kill than someone loudly accusing one evil role.

Endgame priority: preserve the winning vote

When the lobby is small, every target choice should be translated into the next survivor count. Protection, kill and control targets that do not affect parity may be objectively worse than less obvious choices that preserve or remove the deciding vote.

Ranking three possible targets

A Doctor must choose between a confirmed Sheriff, an unrevealed Mayor claim and a quiet Town Support claim. If Mafia needs to remove the Sheriff’s result chain to keep two fake claims alive, the Sheriff is the likely attack. If that Mayor claim becomes a public reveal, remove the Mayor from the Doctor’s viable healing choices and reconsider who supplies the evidence for the Mayor’s vote.

Compare the decisions a result would change

A Sheriff choosing between three candidates
Candidate Potential value Main limitation
A revealed Mayor Little new alignment information in an ordinary setup Does not resolve the disputed claims
One of two claims competing for a slot An informative result may focus tomorrow’s trial Framing and detection exceptions remain possible
An unclaimed quiet player Can add information when no conflict is more urgent Silence alone is not evidence of evil

Choose for the next decision, not simply for the chance to receive a dramatic result. Recalculate after the daytime claims change.

Common mistakes

  • Targeting the loudest player instead of the highest-value player.
  • Investigating already-confirmed roles while counterclaims remain unresolved.
  • Roleblocking passive claims that have no action to stop.
  • Ignoring parity when choosing the final night target.
