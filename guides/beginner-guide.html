Town of Salem Beginner Guide: First Games, Claims & Night Logs

A practical first-match roadmap covering the day/night cycle, role claims, wills, voting, and how to turn scattered events into useful evidence.

These Town of Salem tips are deliberately procedural: record facts first, then decide what they mean.

Quick answer: Start by learning the match rhythm, not by memorizing every role. Your first goal is to preserve what happened each night and explain your own actions without panic.

Your first three matches should have different goals

Match one is about surviving the interface: read the role card, note your win condition, and watch how Night, Discussion and Trial connect. Match two is about writing a useful will with night numbers, targets and results. Match three is about claims: ask yourself what information another player would need to verify your story. This progression is faster than trying to memorize forty-eight roles before you understand how evidence moves through a game.

Build a last will before you need it

Write the role name and a simple chronological log as soon as the game starts. Investigative roles should preserve exact result wording; protectors should list targets; support roles should record blocks, transports or other actions. A will is not decoration—it is the record that survives your death and lets Town test later claims against earlier information.

Know when to speak

Useful information ages quickly. A Sheriff result posted only after the target is on trial looks less credible than the same result posted at the start of the day. At the same time, not every role should reveal immediately. Ask whether speaking now protects Town from a bad vote, confirms another player, or merely gives killers a better target.

Treat the role list as a constraint

Do not evaluate claims one at a time. If three players claim roles that cannot all fit the setup, the conflict matters even when each individual story sounds plausible. Graveyard flips, confirmed reveals and cleaned deaths all change how much claim space remains.

Vote with the next night in mind

Before a late-game guilty vote, count how many players survive and which faction can still kill. A suspicious player may be evil, but a rushed elimination can still hand parity to a different faction. Beginners improve quickly when they ask “what does the board look like after this vote?” rather than “who feels worst right now?”

A simple beginner example

You are Sheriff. Night 1 you check Player 6 and receive an innocent result. Write “N1 – 6 – innocent” immediately. On Day 2, post the result without calling Player 6 confirmed Town, because roles such as Godfather can complicate Sheriff clears. If Player 6 later makes a claim that conflicts with the role list, your old result is still useful—but it is one clue, not the entire case.

Common mistakes

  • Trying to memorize every role before learning the day/night loop.
  • Keeping results private until the moment you are accused.
  • Calling an innocent investigative result a hard confirmation.
  • Voting without reading the graveyard and remaining role slots.
