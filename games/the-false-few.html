The False Few

Investigate a lineup of suspects, spend limited abilities carefully and work out which criminals are posing as innocent people.

Solo · hidden criminals

Play The False Few

Ready when you are.

Single-player browser game. Use the mouse to investigate, A and D to move through photos, and Escape or Space for the pause menu and control settings.

Work from abilities to identities

The False Few is a solo investigation built around suspects whose apparent roles may conceal criminals. It combines randomized cases with a limited supply of useful abilities. Your task is to turn those abilities into enough evidence to identify the criminals, rather than choosing the least friendly-looking portrait.

The connection to Salem is the gap between a claimed identity and what that identity can actually establish. A role label is a starting point for a question. Ask what the ability measures, what it leaves unknown and whether you are relying on a person you have not yet cleared.

Choose a question before spending an ability

A useful approach is to name two explanations that still fit the evidence, then look for an action that separates them. Rechecking something you already believe may feel reassuring while leaving the important uncertainty untouched. A result is most valuable when it changes which suspects remain possible.

For example, imagine a general deduction problem in which either Alex or Blair is lying. Evidence about an unrelated person may establish a new fact without settling that dispute. A check that rules out one of the two existing stories gives the next decision a firmer basis. This is a reasoning example, not a fixed solution to a generated case.

Keep a small evidence ledger

Separate observations, assumptions and remaining possibilities. “The ability reported this” and “therefore this suspect is innocent” belong on different lines. If an accusation fails, revisit the inference that joined them. That habit makes a failed case more useful than immediately trying another arbitrary selection.

The same discipline helps when writing a Town of Salem last will: preserve the actual result before adding your interpretation. In a live lobby, other players need to be able to inspect that distinction too.

Start with the tutorial

The game includes a tutorial and configurable controls. Use the opening instruction to learn how a particular ability is phrased before treating its output as universal proof. A and D move through the photos; the pause menu provides the control settings. There is no multiplayer lobby to organize here.

Where the comparison with Salem stops

A programmed case does not adapt its testimony when you put pressure on it. Human opponents in Salem can coordinate, deliberately give a true result or change their cover story. Use this game to practise careful elimination, then return to claiming strategy for the communication and persuasion that a real Town vote also needs.
