🎲 Setups change the solve

Town of Salem game modes

Classic, Ranked Practice, All Any, Coven and Custom games reward different kinds of reasoning. The role list determines how much certainty you can extract from a claim.

Choose the right setup

Classic Mode Guide

Classic mode uses a more structured role list, making counterclaims and Investigator results easier to interpret for beginners.

Ranked Practice Guide

Ranked Practice provides a structured setting for learning claims, night logs, role-list accounting and coordinated votes before entering Ranked.

All Any Guide

All Any removes many of the role-list constraints that make Classic solving straightforward, so behavior and interaction evidence become more valuable.

Coven Modes Guide

Coven modes expand the role pool and interaction density, changing investigative results, claim safety and night-action interpretation.

Custom Games Guide

Custom setups can rewrite the normal role-list assumptions, so solve the host’s configured list before treating any claim as impossible.

Why the mode matters

In a structured role list, a claim can become impossible because every slot is accounted for. In All Any, the same claim may remain plausible much longer because duplicates and unusual combinations are possible. Coven modes add extra roles and interactions, while Custom games can invalidate assumptions you use in normal queues.

Before evaluating a Town of Salem role claim, always identify the mode and read the configured role list. This single habit prevents many false counterclaims and bad executions.

Choose a mode by how much structure you want

Classic teaches role-list logic because the setup is comparatively readable. Ranked Practice rewards disciplined claims and vote math. All Any creates wider claim space and more unusual faction mixes. Coven adds expansion mechanics, while Custom lets the host design a specific puzzle.

Why the same strategy changes by mode

A double role claim that is impossible in a fixed setup may be completely legal in All Any. An Investigator table remembered from Classic can be wrong in Coven. Good players identify the mode first, then decide how much weight to give role-list constraints, investigative groups and counterclaims.

Best mode for learning

New players usually benefit from a structured role list because each flip teaches how slots close. After the basics feel comfortable, All Any and Coven are useful for learning uncertainty, broader role interactions and how to reason when the setup itself is less informative.

Ranked and Ranked Practice

Ranked is the competitive queue; Ranked Practice provides a place to learn its style of structured claims and coordinated voting. Read the current role list in both. A remembered season’s setup is not sufficient to disprove a claim if the list has changed.

Practice keeping an up-to-date will, answering a role request directly and explaining the next vote from evidence. Queue access requirements and ranking belong to the current client. Finishing a practice game does not by itself guarantee that an account meets every Ranked requirement.

Town Traitor

In Town Traitor, a player with a Town role secretly helps the hostile faction. The ability can be genuine while the allegiance is hostile: a correct heal, investigation or public reveal cannot solve every loyalty question. Watch how a player uses information and which outcomes their votes support.

The final hunt has a countdown and mode-specific rules. Read the timer and count the remaining trials rather than relying on the usual assumption that Town can wait indefinitely. The Coven Town Traitor section explains the expansion variant.

Dracula’s Palace

Dracula’s Palace centers the Vampire conversion threat and the roles that interact with it. A player’s earlier alignment may not describe their current objective after conversion. Keep the timing of checks and deaths, and learn the Vampire Hunter interaction before treating a successful Vampire check as general Town confirmation.

Rainbow, Rapid and other special lists

Rainbow and Rapid are special-format names associated with the original game’s mode history. They are not substitutes for reading a present lobby: a special list can concentrate dangerous roles or change how much time a player has to discuss and act. Use Custom to inspect the actual setup instead of assuming that every named historical queue is currently available.

For VIP, Lovers and the expansion’s open setup, use the Coven mode guide. Mode availability and rotation can change; the visible client lobby determines what you can join now.
